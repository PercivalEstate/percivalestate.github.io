About

About the Percival Estate Community

We are a non-profit community organisation representing residents of the Percival Estate in the London Borough of Islington; we help to give a voice to residents and support with making improvements to the estate.

We're in the process of establishing a formal Tenants and Residents Association (TRA). Constituting a TRA is helpful for the community as this will enable us to access specialised funding pots and other opportunities that can make our estate a better place to live, for everyone.

If you're a tenant or leaseholder that is part of the estate and you're interested in learning more and would like to become a member of the community group, please register to join and we'll be in touch in due course.

History of the Percival Estate

The Percival Estate is an Islington Council estate in Clerkenwell so named after its neighbouring street to the north side - Percival Street - records show this was originally spelled as 'Perceval Street', named after a former Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, Spencer Perceval. The wider footprint of the estate is enclosed by Compton Street on the south side, Agdon Street to the west and neighbours The Triangle Estate to the east.

The estate was constructed in phases; it has a layered history tied to redevelopment efforts in the 20th century.

Cyrus House (1933–1934)

Built by Finsbury Borough Council and designed by E. C. P. Monson in a traditional style influenced by earlier housing projects. The five-storey block contains 40 two- and three-bedroom flats, with balcony access from a central courtyard reached via a striking two-storey archway. It was part of a redevelopment enabled by the Northampton Estate's discounted land offer and revised housing subsidies. The project included extending Malta Street (Malta Street has since been truncated and the southern portion incorporated into Compton Street). The construction was handled by Gee, Walker & Slater Ltd.

Tompion House & Earnshaw House (1946–1949)

Designed by Messrs Joseph in 1938 and built by W. H. Gaze & Sons Ltd for the London County Council (LCC). These two six-storey blocks were named after horologists Thomas Tompion and Thomas Earnshaw.

Grimthorpe House (1950–1952)

Designed by John Partridge under the LCC Architect's Department and approved in 1949, Grimthorpe House was built by A. E. Symes Ltd. This eight-storey Modernist block with 128 flats reflects the LCC's post-war Modernist aspirations and is named after Lord Grimthorpe, long-serving president of the British Horological Institute. The building features dramatic glazed stairwells and a facade clad in hand-made stock bricks. It was substantially refurbished by Islington Council in 1984–5.

North-west view of Grimthorpe House (1952)

Crayle House (1959–1960)

Designed by the LCC Architect's Department and built by Stewart & Partners Ltd on the site of a former factory. Initially comprising 20 maisonettes, the four-storey block with balcony access was altered in 1983–1984. It was likely named after 17th-century London watchmakers Richard and William Crayle.

Partridge Court (1976–1977)

Added by the Greater London Council (GLC) and designed in 1972 by Renton Howard Wood Levin Partners, with Ove Arup & Partners as engineers. This five-storey block houses 13 flats and maisonettes, including facilities for elderly residents. It is named after an old Clerkenwell family.

Each construction highlights the evolution of public housing in Clerkenwell, from pre-war traditional styles to post-war Modernist approaches, reflecting changing priorities and architectural trends.

Safety

Council tenants living on the estate must abide by the Conditions of Tenancy set by Islington Council. The council must ensure the properties they manage are suitable for habitation by their tenants. As an example, damp and mould issues can be reported to Islington Council repairs team. Communal areas are managed by Islington Council Estate Services and cleaned by the caretaking team. Private tenants have their own contracts with their landlords and their own rights and responsibilities according to law. Landlords must ensure that their properties meet necessary safety standards to be suitable for habitation for their tenants. Leaseholders must ensure they follow the conditions of their lease.

Anti-social behaviour (ASB) is not permitted on the estate. You can report ASB to Islington Council. You can report criminal activity to the police. If there is an emergency or someone is at immediate risk, call 999. The non-emergency number is 101. You can also report in-person at the front desk of Islington Police Station. You can also keep your local Bunhill Safer Neighbourhoods Team informed via:
SNTNI-Bunhillat met dot police dot uk

You can get more involved with community policing by attending the local Bunhill ward panel, which gives residents an opportunity to share their community safety concerns with the Metropolitan Police and Islington Council's Community Safety Team. You can also apply to take part in a Ride Along scheme or other Community Observer Initiatives. Community Weapon Sweeps are also a great way to support local policing. The closest knife bin is on Central Street, outside Finsbury Leisure Centre.

Be aware of your nearest defibrillator and your nearest A&E.

Fire risk assessments for high-rise buildings are soon to be published online via the building safety system, Twinnedit, tenants and leaseholders who do not live in a high-rise building can access the FRA for their building via the Islington Council website or by emailing FireSafety@islington.gov.uk.

In the event of property damage, leaseholders are able to claim on their buildings insurance from Protector Insurance which is paid for via their service charge. Leaseholders can claim via the insurance by filing a First Notification of Loss via protectorclaims@uk.sedgwick.com.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 August 2026

Who we are

The Percival Estate Community is an unincorporated non-profit community organisation representing residents of the Percival Estate in the London Borough of Islington. We are the data controller for the personal information described in this policy. You can contact us at tra@percivalestate.com or at Tompion Community Hall, 40 Percival Street, London, EC1V 0EB.

This policy explains what personal information we collect through https://percivalestate.com/ and what we do with it. We handle your information in line with the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.

What we collect

When you register as a member. Through our registration form we collect your name, your email address, the building and flat you live in, and whether you are a council tenant, leaseholder or private tenant. Some fields are optional, and you can leave them blank.

When you contact us. If you use our contact form or email us, we collect your name, your email address and whatever you choose to write in your message.

When you simply visit. We do not use analytics and we do not track visitors. Our web host and the third parties listed below receive your IP address as an ordinary part of serving the page to you.

Why we use it, and our lawful basis

  • To run the community organisation — keeping a record of members, organising meetings, and representing residents to Islington Council and other bodies. Our lawful basis is your consent, which you give by choosing to register.
  • To send you community updates about the estate and the organisation. Our lawful basis is your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time by emailing us.
  • To reply to you when you get in touch. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in responding to people who contact us.

We do not sell your information, and we do not use it to make automated decisions about you.

Who else handles your information

We use a small number of third-party services to run this website. They process your information on our behalf:

  • Airtable — stores our membership records and powers the forms and tables embedded on this site. Airtable is based in the United States, so your information is transferred outside the UK and protected by the safeguards in Airtable's own data processing terms.
  • Form Taxi — delivers messages sent through our contact form to us by email.
  • GitHub Pages — hosts this website and keeps standard server logs, which include visitors' IP addresses.
  • Font Awesome — supplies the icons used on this site from its content delivery network, which receives your IP address when the page loads.

Members of the Percival Estate Community committee can see membership records so that they can carry out their roles. We will also disclose information if the law requires it.

How long we keep it

We keep your membership record for as long as you are a member. If you ask us to remove you, or you tell us you have left the estate, we will delete your record. Messages sent through the contact form are kept only for as long as we need them to deal with what you wrote to us about.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of the information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct anything that is wrong;
  • ask us to delete your information;
  • ask us to restrict or stop using it, or object to how we are using it;
  • withdraw your consent at any time, which will not affect anything we did before you withdrew it.

To do any of these, email tra@percivalestate.com. These rights are free to use and we will respond within one month.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/ or on 0303 123 1113. We would appreciate the chance to put things right first.

Cookies

This website does not set any cookies of its own, and we do not use analytics or advertising cookies. The Airtable forms and tables embedded on our pages may set their own cookies when they load; you can control these through your browser settings.

Other websites

Our pages link to other websites, including Islington Council, the Metropolitan Police and Airtable. We are not responsible for their content or their privacy practices, and this policy does not cover them.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top of this page. If the change is significant, we will tell members by email.
